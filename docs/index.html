Bearings.

Get your bearings: the compass, where you are, the time, your coordinates and elevation, the sun and moon, and a map. All on one screen.

Download Open in your browser
Free and open source. No ads, no account, no tracking. Made by Bradley Cowdrey.

Get Bearings.

iPhone and iPad

iOS 17 or later

Install with TestFlight

  1. Open this link on your iPhone or iPad, install Apple’s free TestFlight app if asked, then tap Install.
  2. TestFlight installs each new version as it comes out. The App Store version is on its way.
  3. Add the widgets: touch and hold the Home Screen or Lock Screen, tap Edit, then add Bearings.

Or sideload Bearings.ipa with AltStore or Sideloadly.

Android

Android 8.0 or later · no Google Play services needed

Download for Android

  1. Download Bearings.apk on your phone and open it.
  2. If Android asks, allow your browser to install apps, then tap Install.
  3. Add the widget: touch and hold the Home Screen, tap Widgets, then Bearings.

Mac

macOS 14 or later · Apple silicon and Intel

Download for Mac

  1. Open Bearings.dmg and drag Bearings into Applications.
  2. The first time, Control-click Bearings and choose Open. On macOS 15 or later, if it won’t open, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Add the desktop widgets: right-click the desktop, Edit Widgets, then search Bearings. Macs have no compass, so the dial stays north-up.

Web

Any modern browser · nothing to install

Open Bearings

  1. Open it on your phone; allow location and, on iPhone, motion when asked.
  2. Add it to your Home Screen from the browser’s Share menu.
  3. On a computer it shows your position, the sun and the map.

Everything on one screen.

  • A clear compass. 228° SW, true or magnetic north with the declination and the accuracy, turning the short way past north, with a tick at each point.
  • Where and when. The town you’re in, or how far you are from the nearest one, with the date and time, on a bar the colour of the sky above you.
  • Four layouts. Small, Medium, Large and Extra Large: from a compass with a big map to the biggest compass with one summary row. Medium is the standard.
  • Six coordinate formats. Decimal degrees, DMS, decimal minutes, UTM, MGRS and Plus Codes. Tap to switch; hold to copy.
  • Elevation in feet and metres at once, from GPS or terrain data, with air pressure where your phone has a barometer.
  • The sun and moon on the dial, with sunrise, sunset and the moon’s phase.
  • Targets. Lock a bearing and see how far you’ve turned from it; mark a spot (where you parked, or camp) and see its direction and distance. Both in one card.
  • A map with your heading, north up or following you, that opens larger with a tap. Satellite on Apple devices.
  • Compass widgets. The dial with the sun, the moon and your marked spot on the Home Screen, the Mac desktop and Android; elevation on the iPhone Lock Screen.
  • Honest accuracy. ± for position and heading, a warning near magnets or metal, a bubble level, and speed as you move.
  • Night mode. The whole screen in red after dark, to keep your night vision.
  • Private and offline. Your location stays on your device; the compass, coordinates, sun and place names work without a connection.

Not a survey instrument.

Phone compasses are thrown off by metal, magnets, cases and cars, and positions and elevations can be wrong. Where it matters, carry a map and a real compass. See the Terms of Use and Privacy.
