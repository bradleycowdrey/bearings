Get your bearings. The compass, where you are, the time, your coordinates and elevation, the sun and moon, and a map, all on one screen.

A clear compass: your heading in degrees and points (228° SW), true north or magnetic north with the declination, and a light tick as you pass N, E, S and W.

Where and when: the town you’re in, or how far you are from the nearest one (“66 mi NE of Montrose, Colorado”), with today’s date and the time.

Coordinates in six formats: decimal degrees, degrees minutes seconds, decimal minutes, UTM, MGRS and Plus Codes. Tap to switch, hold to copy, or share your location.

Elevation in feet and metres at once, from GPS when it’s accurate, otherwise terrain data, with air pressure on iPhones with a barometer.

The sun and moon on the dial, with sunrise, sunset and the moon’s phase.

Mark a spot and find it again: its direction on the dial and how far away it is.

Lock a bearing and see how far you’ve turned from it. A bubble level sits in the middle of the dial.

A map with your heading, north up or following you, in standard or satellite view.

Honest accuracy: ± for position and heading, a figure-8 hint only when the compass needs it, a warning near magnets and metal, and your speed as you move.

Night mode turns everything red to keep your night vision.

Widgets for the Home Screen in small, medium and large, and for the Lock Screen: your place, elevation, coordinates, and the sun and moon.

Works offline, except the map and terrain elevation.

Your location stays on your iPhone. Bearings sends only a position rounded to about 100 m to look up the ground elevation. No account, no ads, no tracking.

Free. Also on the Mac, Android and the web. Open source.
